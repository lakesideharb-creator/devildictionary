Guess the word. Survive the definition.

The Devil’s Dictionary is a free word game. One entry hides in a grid of letters. Hold the first letter and slide — along a row, a column, or a diagonal — until the word is spelled. The dictionary then reads its definition back, and it is rarely the comfortable one.

The title, and fifty-five of the definitions, come from Ambrose Bierce’s The Devil’s Dictionary (1911), a public-domain book of cynical definitions. Forty-five more were written for this edition in that same voice. One hundred entries, four stages, and no account to open. Where the definitions come from.

A few of the entries are already written out: Lawyer, Politics, and Telephone. Today’s word changes with the date. There is also a short promo, printable cards for the table, and a community edition for lines written by readers.
